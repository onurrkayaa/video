## Özet

I measured the cause of the earlier "audio slipping" on this Mac on 2026-10-05, read-only. The audio in renders/ilk-montaj-v2.mp4 lines up sample for sample with ilk-montaj/ses/sarki.wav: the lag was 0.00 ms in all 23 windows, with correlation of at least 0.999. ffmpeg scdet found 48 cut events, and 46 of them fall exactly on the planned frames. So the renderer was accurate. The fault was the plan itself: ilk-montaj/kurgu.py used a fixed 76.005 BPM grid on a song played by people, whose tempo drifts. Two existing tools make the same mistake. `hyperframes beats` uses bpm-detective and then regularizeBeats(), which lays a constant grid and forces the BPM into 60–120. The music-to-video analyzer uses librosa beat_track with one tempo for the whole song.

Recommended stack, all local with no accounts:
- **Primary tracker:** Beat This! 1.1.0 (ISMIR 2024 state of the art; MIT for code and, per the maintainers on 2026-05-28, for weights). It gives a time for each beat plus downbeats and follows tempo drift. It cannot be used alone: on the hard SMC set of soft, slow, expressive music it scores F=0.627 and its activations are often "confident but wrong".
- **Committee:** Essentia 2.1b6 RhythmExtractor2013 multifeature (AGPL; a signal-processing tracker with a built-in confidence from 0 to 5.32), plus Beat This! runs with final0, final1 and final2, plus an optional run on the Demucs no_vocals stem.
- **Agreement metrics:** mir_eval 0.8.2, using F-measure at ±70 ms and Information Gain. A mean mutual agreement of 1.5 bits or more was judged perceptually acceptable by listeners (Zapata et al., ISMIR 2012).
- **librosa 1.0:** onsets in SuperFlux style; smoothing Beat This! activations with a tempo that can vary (no DBN needed); self-similarity structure; PNG plots the agent can read.
- **Vocal timing:** Demucs 4.1.0 vocals stem, then mlx-whisper with large-v3-turbo and condition_on_previous_text=False, for vocal phrase starts and word timing.
- **Render checks:** ffmpeg scdet plus an FFT cross-correlation script.

Acceptance rules:
- Hard cuts on beats are allowed only if all of these hold: committee F ≥ 0.85, Information Gain ≥ 1.5 bits, Essentia confidence > 1.5, F between the three Beat This! checkpoints ≥ 0.95, and the median offset between trackers over every 8 beats stays within ±25 ms.
- Each hard cut needs trackers agreeing within 35 ms and an onset peak within 35 ms. Round the cut frame down so that −1 frame < cut − beat ≤ 0. The reason is asymmetric tolerance: ITU-R BT.1359 detectability is 45 ms with audio early and 125 ms with audio late; EBU R37 allows +40/−60 ms.
- The render must show every hard cut on its planned frame, and an audio lag of at most 1 ms with no more than 1 ms spread across the song.
- If these fail, edit by phrases instead: cut on downbeats, section changes and vocal phrase starts with 0.5–1.5 s dissolves, or ask the user for a one-minute click-track check or tap-along.

Environment blocker: the Xcode license has not been accepted, so Homebrew and /usr/bin/python3 fail. Use the uv 0.12.21 already installed at ~/Library/Python/3.14/bin/uv, with a uv-managed Python 3.12.14 and `--no-build` installs. All core packages have Python 3.12 arm64 wheels. Running with DEVELOPER_DIR=/Library/Developer/CommandLineTools makes clang work without sudo, but brew still refuses.

### Primary beat and downbeat tracking that follows tempo drift and rubato (replaces the fixed 76.005 BPM grid)
- **Seçim:** Beat This! (CPJKU, ISMIR 2024) — PyPI beat-this 1.1.0, checkpoint final0; final1 and final2 as a seed ensemble
- Alternatifler: madmom RNN+DBN (weights non-commercial); allin1 (trained on Harmonix only); Essentia multifeature (signal processing, weaker, but independent)
- Neden: State-of-the-art tracker with no DBN, so there is no tempo or meter prior and it follows drift. GTZAN: beat F1 89.1, downbeat F1 78.3 (~20M params); small0 (~2M params) scores 88.8. Trained on 16 datasets including GuitarSet, ASAP (expressive piano), RWC, Filosax and Candombe. Frames are 50 fps (hop 441 at 22.05 kHz), so beat times sit on a 20 ms lattice. It returns a time for each beat plus downbeats (not a grid), and the CLI can save raw activations as .npy. Weakness (2026 evidence): on SMC it scores F=0.627, CMLt=0.514, AMLt=0.610, and peak activation is 0.998 on good tracks versus 0.931 on failed ones, so activations are no confidence measure; always gate with a committee. PyPI 1.1.0 was released 2026-04-14. In issue #16 (2026-05-28) the maintainers state the MIT license also applies to the weights.

### Independent second beat tracker with a built-in reliability score (committee member and confidence gate)
- **Seçim:** Essentia 2.1b6 RhythmExtractor2013(method='multifeature'), i.e. BeatTrackerMultiFeature
- Alternatifler: librosa beat_track (weaker, single tempo); madmom RNN (non-commercial weights)
- Neden: A signal-processing tracker combining 5 onset functions through TempoTapMaxAgreement. Its confidence is the agreement among its own candidates: [0,1) very low, [1,1.5] low, (1.5,3.5] good (~80% AMLt), (3.5,5.32] excellent. That is a measurable 'can this song be tracked' signal independent of any neural net. Its failure modes differ from Beat This!, so agreement between the two is meaningful (the MMA method of Zapata/Holzapfel). Input must be 44.1 kHz; tempo range defaults to 40–208 BPM.

### Quantitative agreement metrics and click sonification (verification without listening)
- **Seçim:** mir_eval 0.8.2
- Alternatifler: own numpy implementations (avoid; not comparable to the literature)

## Kaçınılacaklar

- **hyperframes beats (hyperframes 0.8.124)** — The local code (node_modules/hyperframes/dist/beat-analyzer.global.js) finds energy peaks, estimates BPM with bpm-detective, then regularizeBeats() lays a fixed grid (t += 60/bpm) and forces the BPM into 60–120. That is the same failure as the 76.005 BPM grid. Use its file format only for Studio display, by writing Beat This! times into beats/<audio>.json ({version:1, audio, beats:[{time, strength}]}).
- **Any fixed-BPM grid (ilk-montaj/kurgu.py VURUS = 60/76.005; bpm/beats_sec from music-to-video analyze-beatgrid.py on calm songs)** — Songs played by people drift, so a fixed grid builds up hundreds of ms of error (the earlier windows ran from -390 to +300 ms). analyze-beatgrid.py uses librosa.beat.beat_track with one tempo for the whole song, and its own SKILL.md calls the result unreliable on calm music. Keep its energy, onset and phrase fields; replace its beats with the committee result.
- **Home-made numpy spectral-flux + autocorrelation beat tracker** — It already failed on a soft acoustic song, gives no confidence measure, and cannot be compared with published results.
- **BeatNet 1.1.3** — PyPI pins numba==0.54.1 (Python 3.9 or older) and needs madmom and PyAudio. Last release 2023-11-28; built for real-time use; no offline advantage over Beat This!.
- **allin1 from PyPI as published (1.1.0)** — Unmaintained since 2023-10. NATTEN install and API breaks on macOS arm64 (issues #11, #23, #28, #30). Only usable through the unmerged PR #39 branch.
- **SongFormer (ASLP-lab, 2025)** — Tested on NVIDIA L40 / CUDA with Python 3.10. Its MuQ backbone weights are CC-BY-NC 4.0 (Tencent), so it is effectively non-commercial, and it is heavy for a 16 GB M2.
- **madmom RNN/CNN models, Essentia TensorFlow models, MMS-based aligners (facebook/mms-*, torchaudio MMS_FA, ctc-forced-aligner defaults) for WORK outputs** — Their weights are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 or CC-BY-NC 4.0: personal projects only.
- **WhisperX as the default speech-recognition path** — It pins torch~=2.8.0, torchaudio~=2.8.0 and torchvision, and pulls in pyannote-audio>=4, transformers and torchcodec: a ~2 GB environment plus a ~1.2 GB Turkish alignment model. It runs CPU-only on Mac (CTranslate2). Use it only in its own venv when wav2vec2 forced alignment is specifically needed, and never use --diarize (requires a Hugging Face token, i.e. an account).
- **Parakeet, or the default model of hyperframes transcribe, for Turkish** — Parakeet TDT v3 covers 25 European languages and not Turkish. hyperframes transcribe defaults to small.en, which translates non-English audio into English.
- **Whisper with condition_on_previous_text=True on songs** — Added 28 WER points in the 2026 Mac lyrics benchmark, through repetition loops and hallucinated text in instrumental passages.
- **Beat This! --dbn or a madmom DBN with the default min_bpm=55 on slow songs** — The 2026 SMC failure analysis found the 55 BPM floor blocks the correct tempo on 21% of SMC tracks. Lower it to 30 when using a DBN.
- **Treating neural activation height (or the tracker's own confidence) as reliability** — Activations are often confident but wrong: peak activation is 0.998 on well-tracked songs versus 0.931 on completely failed ones. Use agreement between trackers instead.
- **audio-offset-finder 0.5.5** — MFCC-based with ~10 ms accuracy and a numpy<2 pin. Sample-level FFT cross-correlation is simpler and accurate to under 1 ms (checked).
- **aubio (pip 0.4.9, source only, 2019)** — GPL-3 and needs compiling. A Homebrew bottle exists but brew is blocked. Beat This! and librosa supersede it here.
- **Analyzing the compressed source (MP3/AAC) while the renderer decodes it separately** — Different decoders and encoder padding can shift timing by ~20–40 ms (allin1 README). Decode once to a WAV master and use that file everywhere.

## Teknikler

### Audio is the clock: one decoded master file
Decode the song once: ffmpeg -nostdin -i song.m4a -map 0:a:0 -vn -ac 2 -ar 48000 -c:a pcm_s24le master.wav. Use master.wav for all analysis and as the composition's <audio> source. Make derived copies (44.1 kHz mono for Essentia) with ffmpeg. Never analyze the MP3/AAC directly or decode it a second time for the render.

**Doğrulama:** xcorr_check.py between master.wav and every derived copy and the final render: |lag| ≤ 1 ms in every window, spread ≤ 1 ms, correlation ≥ 0.9. ffprobe: audio start_time 0.000 and |audio duration − video duration| ≤ 1/fps.

### Committee beat tracking with an agreement gate (MMA/MaxMA)
Run Beat This! final0, final1 and final2 on master.wav, plus Essentia multifeature; optionally Beat This! on the Demucs no_vocals stem and a madmom DBN (min_bpm=30) over the Beat This! activations. For every pair compute mir_eval f_measure (0.07 s), information_gain (41 bins) and continuity (AMLt). MMA = mean Information Gain across pairs; the member with the highest mean agreement (MaxMA) becomes the beat list. For songs with a long intro, compute on the full arrays rather than mir_eval's default 5 s trim.

**Doğrulama:** HIGH (beat cuts allowed): F(Beat This!, Essentia) ≥ 0.85, MMA ≥ 1.5 bits, Essentia confidence > 1.5, F between the Beat This! checkpoints ≥ 0.95. MEDIUM: F 0.6–0.85 or 1.0–1.5 bits, so cut only on beats where the trackers agree locally. LOW: anything worse, so use the fallback. The 1.5-bit and Essentia bands come from the literature and docs; the F thresholds are engineering choices to tune on a few songs.

### Metrical level (octave) check
Compare median beat intervals across the committee. If the ratio is about 2 or 0.5, pick the level whose tempo lies in 60–140 BPM and whose downbeats line up with section boundaries and vocal phrase starts. Record the choice in the analysis JSON.

**Doğrulama:** After the choice, the median interval ratio between members is within 0.97–1.03, and each member's AMLt is close to its CMLt (a large gap means the trackers are on different metrical levels).

### Local phase stability (replaces the failed 10-second offset test)
For each beat of the chosen list, find the nearest beat of each other member and record the difference. Take a rolling median and MAD over 8 beats. Also build a beat-aligned raster: the onset envelope from −0.5 to +0.5 beat interval around every beat, one row per beat, plus the phase concentration of onset peaks.

**Doğrulama:** In every window used for cuts: |median difference| ≤ 25 ms, change between windows ≤ 20 ms, and no difference > 70 ms at a cut. The raster's bright stripe stays vertical at phase 0; a slanted stripe means drift. Regions that fail are marked as phrase-only.

### Smoothing the beat sequence without a DBN
Take Beat This! --activations (.npy, 50 fps), apply a sigmoid, and pass it as onset_envelope to librosa.beat.beat_track(sr=22050, hop_length=441, bpm=<per-frame tempo from an 8-beat median of the raw beat intervals>, tightness 100–400, trim=False). Or use madmom's DBNBeatTrackingProcessor(min_bpm=30, fps=50) and tune transition_lambda per song.

**Doğrulama:** F between the smoothed list and raw Beat This! ≥ 0.9. Beats are inserted or removed only where the raw interval ratio fell outside 0.7–1.4. The tempo curve has no jumps above ~8% between neighboring beats unless the music really changes.

### Choosing cut points
A beat or downbeat is a candidate only if:
- the committee agrees within ±35 ms;
- an onset peak lies within ±35 ms (librosa onset_strength, hop 128, lag=2, max_size=3);
- it does not fall inside a sung word (at least 50 ms clear of word interiors from Whisper on the vocal stem).
Prefer downbeats, phrase starts and section changes. In slow songs cut every 2–4 bars, not every beat; in energetic sections cut every 1–2 beats.

**Doğrulama:** A table for every cut showing tracker agreement, onset distance and word overlap. 100% of hard cuts meet the first two conditions; any that fail become dissolves or move to the nearest beat that qualifies.

### Round cut frames down, so picture comes slightly early
cut_frame = floor(beat × fps + 1e-6). Render at 60 fps when hard cuts are dense. Reason: ITU-R BT.1359 detectability is 45 ms when audio leads and 125 ms when it lags, and EBU R37 allows +40/−60 ms, so a cut slightly before the beat is safer than one after it.

**Doğrulama:** For every hard cut, −1 frame < cut − beat ≤ 0, computed from the planned cut list before rendering.

### Anchor transitions and motion to the beat
Hard cut exactly on the beat. Dissolve: the 50%-opacity midpoint on the beat, length ≥ 1 beat (≥ 12 frames) in slow songs. Whip or flash: the peak frame on the beat. Zoom punch: the peak scale on the beat, with the ease starting 2–4 frames earlier. Slow-motion ramps start and end on downbeats. Ken Burns moves span whole bars.

**Doğrulama:** Compute event times from the GSAP timeline or cut list and diff them against the beat list (≤ 1 frame). hyperframes snapshot --describe false at the beat frames confirms the visual peak.

### Render verification
After rendering, run ffmpeg scdet (scale=96, threshold≈12, sc_pass=1, showinfo) to list hard cuts, and run xcorr_check.py with master.wav against the render. Measured baseline on ilk-montaj-v2: 46/48 cuts exactly on planned frames; audio lag 0.00 ms in 23 windows.

**Doğrulama:** 100% of planned hard cuts detected within 1 frame of their planned time. Audio lag |·| ≤ 1 ms and spread ≤ 1 ms across the song. No unplanned cut events except known flashes or dissolves.

### Diagnostic images the agent can read
Use matplotlib to save one PNG per 15–20 s window: log-mel spectrogram and onset envelope; Beat This! beats as solid lines, Essentia as dashed, downbeats thick, planned cuts red; Whisper word spans shaded. Also save the beat-aligned raster, offset-over-time and tempo-curve plots. Open them with Read.

**Doğrulama:** The raster stripe is vertical at phase 0; offsets stay inside the ±25 ms band; red cut lines sit on corroborated onsets. Any visual anomaly gets a re-check with the numbers.

### Click preview for the human
Make 30–60 s stereo excerpts of the lowest-agreement windows with mir_eval.sonify.clicks: music left, beat clicks right, a distinct click at each cut (the ISMIR 2012 listening-test format). Alternatively the user opens master.wav plus final0.beats in Sonic Visualiser.

**Doğrulama:** The user's yes/no for each excerpt is logged. A 'no' sends that region to the fallback or a tap-along.

### Fallback when confidence is low (phrase-based editing or tap-along)
No hard cuts on beats. Cut on section boundaries (self-similarity novelty or allin1) and on vocal phrase starts (vocal-stem RMS activity plus the first Whisper words), with 0.5–1.5 s dissolves that hide ±100 ms errors; motion is continuous rather than pulsing. Or a local HTML tap-along: the user presses space on each beat for two passes. The taps choose the metrical level and phase only; the exact times come from snapping to Beat This! activation peaks within ±60 ms, with the median tap offset used as latency calibration.

**Doğrulama:** The two tap passes agree with F ≥ 0.9 at ±70 ms. The snapped beats pass the local phase stability check. Dissolve midpoints land on downbeats within 1 frame.

### Vocal and lyric timing
Separate with demucs --two-stems=vocals. Then mlx_whisper.transcribe on vocals.wav with large-v3-turbo, the language set explicitly, word_timestamps=True, condition_on_previous_text=False, hallucination_silence_threshold≈2, and initial_prompt=lyrics if known. Snap word starts to onset peaks in the vocal stem within ±150 ms. With known lyrics, use stable-ts align() (CPU) or WhisperX wav2vec2 alignment (Turkish model CC-BY-4.0) in a separate venv.

**Doğrulama:** Two independent decodes (turbo vs large-v3, or MLX vs wav2vec2 alignment) agree on ≥ 90% of the word starts used as anchors (mir_eval.alignment.percentage_correct, 0.3 s window). Every word falls inside a segment where the vocal stem is active; any word in a silent stretch is treated as a hallucination.

### Music edits on downbeats
Shorten or lengthen a song by removing or repeating whole bars between downbeats of the same section type, with a 20–50 ms equal-power crossfade (ffmpeg acrossfade c1=qsin:c2=qsin). Small tempo fits up to 3% with atempo, larger ones with pedalboard time_stretch. Rerun the committee on the edited master.

**Doğrulama:** The beat interval at each splice is within ±10 ms of its neighbors. F between the edited file's beats and the spliced original beat list ≥ 0.95. Integrated loudness is unchanged within 0.5 LU.

### Studio beat file and environment hygiene
Write the chosen beats as beats/<audio-relative-path>.json in HyperFrames format ({version:1, audio, beats:[{time, strength}]}, where strength is the activation or 1.0 for downbeats) instead of running 'hyperframes beats'. Keep separate uv venvs (muzik on py3.12; essentia on py3.14; whisperx only on demand). Install with 'uv pip install --no-build', download checkpoints ahead of time, then set HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1.

**Doğrulama:** The JSON times equal the Beat This! times, with uneven beat intervals visible. 'uv pip list' shows the pinned versions. ~/.venvs/muzik/bin/python -c 'import beat_this, librosa, mir_eval, demucs, mlx_whisper' succeeds. du -sh on the caches is under 4 GB.
