## Özet

Recommendation: keep HyperFrames 0.8.124 as the main engine, but stop using it alone. Pin the exact version. Around it, add two layers: (a) ffmpeg (the ffmpeg-static 6.0 build already installed) to prepare clips before composing and to check results after rendering; (b) a Python 3.13 virtual environment for analysis, using beat_this for beat and downbeat times and PySceneDetect plus ffprobe for choosing and ordering shots. Add Blender 5.2 LTS only for real 3D. Add OpenTimelineIO and Kdenlive only when a person wants to fine-tune the cut by hand. Remotion is the strongest alternative, but it can only be used for personal or solo work: once the user works for (or with) more than 3 people, it needs a paid license ($25 per month per creator), and in Remotion 5 contractors count toward that headcount. That breaks the no-paid-services rule.

Evidence measured locally on 2026-10-04/05:
(1) In HyperFrames, data-playback-rate below 1 picks source frames at the output frame rate before slowing down. A 60 fps source at 0.5x rendered at 30 fps had 29 of 58 frame-to-frame transitions as near-duplicates, so only 15 unique frames per second. Real slow motion therefore has to be made in ffmpeg first. A minterpolate test had only 3 of 118 near-duplicates and ran at about 6.5 output fps at 1080x1920.
(2) Audio in HyperFrames is placed to the exact sample: test beeps landed at 1.250, 2.250 and 3.250 s as authored. Video is rounded to whole frames: a 0.25 s data-media-start at 30 fps put the picture 16.7 ms behind the sound. Every time value must be snapped to the frame grid.
(3) The previous montage applied one constant tempo (76.005 BPM, starting at 0.511 s) across about 358 beats. A rough onset check on its song found that, in 30-second windows, the median gap between strong onsets and the grid swung between -137 ms and +102 ms. Usually only 0 to 50 percent of strong onsets were within one frame of the grid. This is a rough check, not ground truth, but it points to the 'sound slipping' complaint: a fixed grid cannot follow a live acoustic song whose tempo drifts. HyperFrames' own beats command (built on bpm-detective with a regularized grid) and music-to-video's librosa grid have the same weakness.
(4) The registry is large: 386 items (164 blocks, 222 components), including 14 shader transitions, whip-pan-cut, beat-freeze-cut, light-leak and flash overlays, camera-shake, an After Effects-style motion-blur component, grain and vignette, and 18 grading presets.

Environment problems found:
- The Xcode license has not been accepted, so brew, swift and clang fail.
- Homebrew's ffmpeg 9.0.2 has no vidstab, zscale, libass or drawtext.
- `brew install melt` installs an unrelated SSH-key tool, and `brew install mlt` pulls in Qt and OpenCV 5 (several GB).
- DaVinci Resolve free cannot be driven by an agent: external scripting is Studio-only, and Python scripting was removed from the free version in 21.1 (Sept 2026).
- The HyperFrames skills tell the agent to run `hyperframes feedback`, which posts to a public channel, after renders.

### Smooth slow motion and ramps from 30 fps phone clips (HyperFrames cannot create in-between frames)
- **Seçim:** Make slow-motion segments before composing. True 60/120/240 fps sources: ffmpeg setpts only. 30 fps sources: ffmpeg minterpolate. Better-quality option: Apple VideoToolbox VTFrameProcessor frame-rate conversion through a small Swift tool.
- Alternatifler: MLT timeremap with image_mode=blend (frame blending, softer); Blender VSE retiming.
- Neden: Measured: HyperFrames data-playback-rate=0.5 left half the output frames as duplicates. Every slow segment must therefore be rendered with all frames unique and placed at rate 1. minterpolate is already installed and passed the duplicate check (2.5 percent). It can bend or tear edges on fast, occluded motion, so check those frames by eye. VTFrameProcessor (WWDC25 session 300; VideoToolbox frame processing) offers machine-learning frame-rate conversion, motion blur, optical flow, temporal noise filtering and super resolution on Apple Silicon, all on the device with no download. It needs a Swift toolchain, which is blocked until the Xcode license is accepted. Another option is RIFE (rife-ncnn-vulkan through MoltenVK, or Practical-RIFE on PyTorch MPS); its weights license is unverified.
- Lisans: kod ffmpeg: GPL. VTFrameProcessor: Apple SDK (proprietary, included with macOS; requires accepting the Xcode/SDK license). · ağırlık VTFrameProcessor models ship with the OS. RIFE weights: unknown, not verified. · ticari: conditional

## Teknikler

### Check and prepare every clip before composing
For each source run: ffprobe -v error -select_streams v:0 -show_entries stream=codec_name,width,height,r_frame_rate,avg_frame_rate,nb_frames,color_transfer:stream_side_data=rotation:format_tags=creation_time -of json. Measure the real frame rate by counting unique frames with mpdecimate (phones often wrap 30 fps content in a 60 fps container). Flag sources at 50 fps or more as slow-motion candidates. Flag arib-std-b67 or smpte2084 as HDR. Stabilize handheld clips in two passes: vidstabdetect=shakiness=5:accuracy=15:result=t.trf, then vidstabtransform=input=t.trf:smoothing=20:zoom=5,unsharp=5:5:0.6. Write H.264 intermediates (CRF 16, -g 15) into project/media. Order shots by creation_time instead of guessing.

**Doğrulama:** Each intermediate: r_frame_rate equals avg_frame_rate; nb_frames equals round(duration*fps); rotation is 0; color_transfer is bt709 for SDR; freezedetect=n=-60dB:d=0.05 reports nothing; a contact sheet per clip looks right.

### Make slow motion beforehand and place it at rate 1
Never use HyperFrames rate below 1 for visible slow motion. For a real 60/120/240 fps source at factor r: -vf "setpts=PTS/r,fps=30". For a 30 fps source: -vf "minterpolate=fps=30/r:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:me_mode=bidir:vsbmc=1,setpts=PTS/r,fps=30" (about 9 s of processing per source second at 1080x1920 on the M2), or VTFrameProcessor frame-rate conversion once Swift works. Speed ramps: join a normal-speed segment and a pre-made slow segment at a motion peak with a 2 to 4 frame blend, or use the beat-freeze-cut block. Use rate lanes only for speed-ups, adding tmix=frames=3 motion blur when the speed-up is beyond 2x.

**Doğrulama:** Duplicate check: ffmpeg -i seg.mp4 -vf "tblend=all_mode=difference,signalstats,metadata=print:key=lavfi.signalstats.YAVG" -f null -. Near-duplicates (YAVG below 0.15 times the median) should be under 3 percent; HyperFrames at rate 0.5 scored 50 percent, minterpolate 2.5 percent. Also look at snapshots of 3 interpolated frames during fast motion for warped edges.

### Plan cuts on real beats and prove there is no drift
Run beat_this to get beat and downbeat times. Pick cut beats by section: in calm verses cut on downbeats or every 2 bars; in choruses every beat or half bar; put hero shots and slow motion on downbeats or song peaks. Snap each time with t = round(beat*fps)/fps (optionally one frame earlier). Keep the music as one continuous audio element. Generate data-start, data-duration and data-media-start from a JSON cut list, all snapped to whole frames.

**Doğrulama:** (1) hyperframes timeline P --json: every video absStart is within half a frame of its planned beat. (2) After rendering, find the actual cuts with ffmpeg -vf "select='gt(scene,0.3)',showinfo" (or scdet): at least 95 percent of hard cuts within one frame of a beat, and the fitted slope of residual against time times duration under one frame (no drift). (3) Cross-correlate the rendered audio with the source song in 10-second windows using numpy FFT: the lag is 0 ± 1 ms in every window, which proves the sound is not slipping.

### Snap every time value to the frame grid
Express data-start, data-media-start and data-duration as whole frames divided by fps. Let animations reach their end state one frame before the clip ends, because visibility runs from start up to, but not including, start+duration. Give overlapping audio elements different data-track-index values and ids.

**Doğrulama:** Run a sync test clip (a white flash plus a 1 kHz beep each second, made with lavfi geq and aevalsrc) through the same pipeline. The flash frame time from signalstats should match the beep onset from silencedetect within 1 ms. Measured without snapping: +16.7 ms.

### Professional transition grammar for montages
Make 70 to 80 percent of changes hard cuts on beats or on motion: cut-the-curve or match-cut, carrying movement direction across the cut. Pick one primary transition: blur-crossfade or a dissolve of 0.5 to 0.8 s with sine.inOut for romantic or calm pieces; whip-pan-cut for energetic ones. Add at most two accents: organic-light-leak-overlay or the light-leak shader at section changes, and flash-through-white once at the climax. Use at most 2 shader transitions per video. Never fade the outgoing scene out before a transition; the outgoing and incoming motion happen at the same moment. End the final scene by dipping to black or white.

**Doğrulama:** Snapshots at T, T+dur/2 and T+dur for every transition, put in a contact sheet; blackdetect=d=0.1:pix_th=0.05 reports only intended black; count the distinct transition types in the composition (3 or fewer).

### Camera moves on real footage
Animate a non-timed inner wrapper (.ic) with GSAP, never the timed video element. Push-in: 1.00 to 1.05–1.08 over the whole shot with ease 'none' or 'sine.inOut'. Punch-in on a beat: 1.0 to 1.2–1.25 in 0.15–0.2 s with expo.out. Ken Burns for photos. Use the camera-shake component at low amplitude only to bring stills to life. Apply the motion-blur component (shutterAngle 180–360) only on whips and slams. Keep digital zoom at 1.2x or less on 1080-wide sources; shoot or use 4K for more. When landscape footage goes into vertical video, use a blurred-fill background or a keyframed crop.

**Doğrulama:** Snapshots at the start, middle and end of each move; hyperframes keyframes diagnostics; blurdetect at the maximum-zoom frame compared with the unzoomed frame: reject the move if blur rises more than about 25 percent (a rule of thumb).

### Shot matching followed by a restrained grade
Measure each clip's signalstats YAVG, SATAVG, UAVG and VAVG on 1 fps samples. Apply hyperframes media-treatment --selector '#cN' --analyze --json, then --grading with a bounded primary correction per clip. Add one global look (warm-daylight, skin-soft or soft-boost at intensity 0.4–0.7) plus finishing (vignette, deterministic grain). Use hyperframes grade-compare for A/B options. Apply a LUT only if the user provides one. Use HyperFrames tone mapping, or a single pre-tonemap pass, for HDR clips.

**Doğrulama:** In the render, the spread of YAVG across shots is lower than in the sources; no clipping (signalstats YMAX at most 235 and YMIN at least 16 in limited range); a contact sheet of every shot at its midpoint shows consistent skin tones and white balance.

### Audio finishing and ducking
Keep the music as one audio element. Bring clip ambience in with data-has-audio and data-automation volume lanes, sitting 18 to 24 dB under the music, and use separate audio elements for J and L cuts. Fade in and out with lanes, not tweens. At the end, run two-pass loudnorm (I=-14 for social, -16 for Apple; TP=-1.5; LRA=11) and remux without re-encoding the video (betikler/ustala.sh already does this).

**Doğrulama:** ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -: integrated loudness within 0.5 LU of the target and true peak at or below -1.0 dBTP; astats shows no clipped samples; ffprobe reports audio and video start_time 0 and durations equal within one frame.

### Rendering without filling the 18 GB disk
export HYPERFRAMES_EXTRACT_CACHE_MAX_MB=1024. Render with --frames-cache-dir <project>/.frames and delete it afterwards. Keep the default (JPEG) frame extraction for camera footage. Deliver with --video-bitrate 8M (or HEVC through --gpu for smaller files). Run hyperframes clean --dry-run, then clean. Delete intermediates once the final is approved, but only after asking.

**Doğrulama:** Compare df -h / before and after each render; du -sh $TMPDIR/hyperframes-extract-cache-* (currently 1.0 GB).

### Use registry effects first, then measure
Before building any named look, filter hyperframes catalog --json by tag (transition, shader, camera, overlay, texture), install with hyperframes add <name>, and wire it in following the hyperframes-registry skill. Read the installed showcase to find the shader's real name, which can differ from the block name. Port a gl-transitions GLSL file only when nothing fits; all 125 are MIT or BSD.

**Doğrulama:** hyperframes lint shows 0 errors; hyperframes check passes; a snapshot is taken at each effect's peak; every component's own duration ends inside its clip window.

- Xcode.app is installed but its license has not been accepted, so brew, swift, clang and node-gyp all fail with 'You have not agreed to the Xcode license'. Blender takes about 1.2 GB, torch for beat_this about 1 GB, Shotcut about 0.6 GB. Run df -h before installs and renders; use --video-bitrate 8M and hyperframes clean.
- HyperFrames changes very quickly (three releases on 2026-10-04 alone), so attributes, skills and defaults shift. Pin 0.8.124 exactly and upgrade deliberately; re-run lint, check and a short test render after each upgrade.
- The HyperFrames skills tell the agent to send `hyperframes feedback` after successful renders ('unless telemetry is disabled'). Add an explicit ban to CLAUDE.md for feedback, --search-miss and --file-issue, alongside the existing cloud, lambda, cloudrun, auth, publish and usage ban, and keep HYPERFRAMES_NO_TELEMETRY=1.
- Frame quantization: any data-start, data-media-start or data-duration that is not a whole number of frames makes the picture lag the sample-exact audio by up to one frame (measured +16.7 ms). Generate all times as k/fps.
- Shader transitions render through html2canvas in preview but blend in Node or in-page WebGL in the final render, so the two can differ. CSS rules apply (explicit .scene background equal to bgColor, no var(), no 'transparent' gradients, no gradients thinner than 4 px). Always snapshot the transition midpoints in the final render.
- iPhone HDR (HLG or Dolby Vision) is tone-mapped to SDR automatically with a generic curve, so colors can differ from the Photos app. Decide SDR or HDR delivery deliberately and measure with signalstats instead of trusting the preview.
- The agent cannot listen or watch in real time. Any claim about sync, beats, smoothness or loudness must come from measurements (cross-correlation lag, cut-versus-beat residuals, tblend duplicate ratio, ebur128). The rough onset check in this report is only a diagnostic, not ground truth.
- License mix: GSAP's 'Standard no-charge' license is proprietary (commercial video is fine; building a visual animation builder product is not). Some registry blocks include GLTF Apple-device models, a trademark issue in commercial work. Remotion's terms change in 5.0. The ffmpeg-static build is GPL and nonfree, so do not redistribute the binary.
- 16 GB RAM: each HyperFrames render worker is a separate Chrome (about 256 MB) plus frame buffers. Four workers is the documented sweet spot; more workers plus Blender or torch at the same time can cause swapping.
